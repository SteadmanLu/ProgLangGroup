A wetland slows water after heavy rain	and gives sediment time to settle. Reeds bend over the river, insects rest on floating leaves, and herons search the shallow water. Volunteers visit each month to record water depth, plant growth, and signs of erosion along the river bank.

In spring the river spreads across a low meadow. The temporary flood carries seeds into quiet pools and leaves fresh soil behind. By summer, water-level markers help the team compare dry weeks with earlier storms. A narrow boardwalk keeps visitors above sensitive roots while still providing a close view of the wetland.

The monitoring report uses the word water for liquid conditions, but water-quality is one hyphenated token. It also distinguishes river from river, because the comma remains attached to the second form.
